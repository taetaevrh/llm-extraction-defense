## Closest-method reading notes

R1 §V/Algorithm3 (PDFpp10–12) already groups prior queries by predicted class, grows reference sets and tests trimmed minimum-distance values with Shapiro–Wilk after sufficient history. Calling class-conditioned semantic distances alone new would be indefensible. [Methods](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.02628)

R5 §3 and AppendixC (PDFpp3–5,14) specify normalized bge-small embeddings, unbiased squared MMD, a benign median bandwidth with four scales, twenty reference draws, and1,000 benign calibration comparisons. Jointly calibrated sequential small-window monitoring is not their reported default; changing window size alone is not novelty. [Methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05725)

R9 §3 (PDFpp4–6) uses victim-derived adversarial contrastive embeddings and an account's accumulated similar-pair count, including filtering/blinding threats. R10 §5 (PDFpp4–5) uses a noisy-outlier/benign VAE and a queue's latent MMD; its two latent classes are explicitly unrelated to target labels. These constrain both recurrence and conditional-detection proposals. [SEAT methods](https://surrealyz.github.io/files/pubs/seat-aisec2021.pdf), [VarDetect methods](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.05166)

R6 §3/Algorithm1 (PDFpp3–6) builds a low-transfer graph using candidate sourcing, filtering and probe checks; suspicious sessions receive follow-up breadcrumbs and graph answers. Its steering assumption is not met by fixed eight-label responses. [Methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15810)

R2 §§3–4 (PDFpp3–4) optimizes poisoned posterior gradient direction under a utility constraint using a proxy Jacobian; R3 §4 (PDFpp4–5) uses keyed deterministic watermark selection and wrong labels. Neither can be presented as a drop-in novel label-only abstention method. [Poisoning methods](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.10908), [DAWN methods](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.00830)

R4 §3.1 (PDFpp2–3) constructs query–posterior supervision for a public pretrained substitute. R8 §4 (PDFpp3–4) separates random sampling from a feedback/bandit selection policy. R7 §§2–3 and AppendicesB–D preserve different extraction objectives and matched controls rather than conflating detection scores with prevention. [BERT methods](https://aclanthology.org/2021.naacl-main.161.pdf), [Knockoff methods](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.02766), [benchmark methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00839v2)

One close unresolved neighbor is **Stealing and Defending the Ends of LLMs / CAP (Coverage-Aware Perturbation)**. A [primary OpenReview indexed submission](https://openreview.net/pdf/c90699239e68f5c9b196e30e759d8f7f50d17d1a.pdf) describes soft-prompt extraction and an active coverage defense, but direct PDF fetches returned403/browser verification. Its displayed status is under review for ICLR2026, not verified acceptance; author attribution in R5 is not independently verified here. Full CAP methods were **not available to read** and it is **not counted among the ten reviewed methods**. Any coverage/credit-allocation novelty claim remains blocked pending that comparison. Do not substitute an unofficial repository for an author's implementation; one SEAT search hit explicitly described itself as unofficial.

**Novelty unresolved.** Class conditioning, sequential histories, semantic encoders, MMD, pair similarity, denial and quotas are established ingredients. A new task, model, threshold or combination does not establish an algorithmic contribution. The hypotheses in the proposal outline identify testable differences, not established originality. Before claiming novelty, obtain inaccessible close methods, expand citation/snowball searches, compare exact algorithms and have the advisor assess the distinction. A rigorous negative result about applicability may be a useful undergraduate contribution, subject to the rubric; no advisor acceptance is presumed.
